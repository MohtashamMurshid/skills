# Narrative blueprints

Choose one sequence, then adapt it to the evidence. Slide counts are defaults, not quotas.

## Investor deck

Use 12–16 slides:

1. **Cover** — company, category, and one memorable market thesis.
2. **Problem** — the costly change or broken workflow, expressed in customer language.
3. **Why now** — technological, regulatory, or behavioral inflection with sourced evidence.
4. **Solution** — what the product makes possible and for whom.
5. **Product flow** — how a user moves from trigger to outcome.
6. **Value** — time, cost, risk, or revenue impact; label modeled values as estimates.
7. **Market** — TAM/SAM/SOM with method, currency, geography, date, and sources.
8. **Traction** — observed revenue, growth, usage, retention, pilots, or pipeline with periods.
9. **Business model** — buyer, pricing unit, gross-margin logic, and expansion path.
10. **Competition** — alternatives and the durable differentiation, not a vanity quadrant.
11. **Go-to-market** — initial wedge, acquisition motion, sales cycle, and expansion.
12. **Roadmap or moat** — what compounds and what remains to be built.
13. **Team** — founder-market fit and key hiring gaps.
14. **Raise** — amount, runway, milestones, and use of funds.

If market or traction numbers are missing, do not fabricate them. Create a clearly labeled “measurement plan” or “data required before external circulation” slide, or omit the claim and flag the gap in the handoff.

## Research deck

Use 10–14 slides:

1. **Cover** — paper title, short contribution statement, authors, affiliation, date.
2. **Research gap** — what existing work fails to capture.
3. **Artifact at a glance** — dataset, model, system, or study dimensions.
4. **Contributions** — three or four precise technical contributions.
5. **Data or source composition** — counts, proportions, provenance, and exclusions.
6. **Method or pipeline** — stages, teacher/model choices, validation, and storage.
7. **Variants or conditions** — experimental groups, localisation modes, or ablations.
8. **Representation or system design** — schema, tool protocol, or architecture.
9. **Quality controls** — record-level and aggregate checks.
10. **Representative example** — one compact input/output, trajectory, or interface.
11. **Evaluation design** — metrics, baselines, splits, and leakage controls.
12. **Results** — only measured findings with uncertainty; omit if not yet evaluated.
13. **Limitations and risks** — synthetic bias, coverage, licensing, external validity, or safety.
14. **Takeaways and next experiment** — what exists now and the next falsifiable milestone.

Keep released training data, validation data, and held-out evaluation assets visually and verbally distinct.

## Hybrid technical/company deck

Use when a research artifact is also a product or platform:

1. Cover thesis
2. User or market problem
3. Technical gap
4. System overview
5. Evidence or benchmark
6. Product workflow
7. Architecture or data moat
8. Adoption or traction
9. Market and buyer
10. Business model
11. Risks and governance
12. Roadmap and ask

Do not let the commercial narrative overstate experimental evidence, and do not let the technical narrative obscure who benefits or pays.

## Headline test

Read only the slide headlines in order. They should form a coherent argument without the body copy. Rewrite headings that merely name a topic—such as “Market” or “Methodology”—into claims supported by the slide.
